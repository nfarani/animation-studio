DAN & THE DANDRUFFS
EPISODE 1: KEEPING THE MONSTERS AWAY
PART 1

You are producing ONLY Part 1 of Episode 1.

IMPORTANT PRODUCTION CONTEXT

Episode 1 will ultimately be told across 5 connected parts.

Each part must:
- work as a standalone publishable mini-episode
- have its own short intro and outro
- end on a satisfying narrative beat or cliffhanger
- preserve continuity for the next part

Later, all 5 parts will also be combined into one longer Episode 1.

Because of this:
- create Part 1 as a complete mini-episode
- also preserve a CLEAN story-only version without repeated intro/outro elements
- do not resolve the full story
- do not introduce material intended for later parts unless needed for setup
- preserve exact continuity of characters, locations, voices, music, visual style and sound design for Parts 2–5

Use all approved Dan & the Dandruffs project canon:
- WORLD_BIBLE
- STYLE_BIBLE
- CHARACTER_BIBLE
- approved references
- approved Dan design
- approved General Arrow design
- approved Scalpetist design
- approved Scalpence Centre design
- approved Tiny Tales logo/series identity

Do not redesign established assets.

MASTER STORY FORMAT:
16:9 horizontal cinematic animation.

Keep key action reasonably centre-safe where practical so strong moments can later be adapted into shorts, but compose primarily for 16:9.

PART 1 TARGET:
Approximately 45–75 seconds depending on natural pacing.

Do not pad unnecessarily.

==================================================
PART 1 PURPOSE
==================================================

Part 1 introduces:

1. Dan as an ordinary human boy.
2. The existence of a hidden civilisation within his hair.
3. The scale and visual identity of that world.
4. The Scalpence Centre.
5. General Arrow.
6. The fact that Dan going to the Green Field creates risk.
7. The threat of Long Foots.

Do NOT explain:
- the entire history of the Burning Tsunamis
- Flake’s parents
- Grandpa Snow’s history
- population theory
- Big Wash mythology
- every aspect of the world

The audience should be intrigued, not lectured.

==================================================
SCENE 1 — DAN
==================================================

EXT. OUTSIDE SCHOOL / STREET — AFTERNOON

Dan leaves school with a few friends.

He is an ordinary kid.

He carries or casually plays with a football while walking.

His friends should behave naturally:
- interrupt one another
- laugh
- steal the ball briefly
- move imperfectly
- backpacks bouncing
- natural child energy

Do not make them feel like actors posing for a commercial.

CAMERA:
relaxed cinematic tracking
human-height
natural lens
slight observational quality

SOUND:
- school ambience
- distant children
- shoes on pavement
- football bouncing
- birds
- light traffic
- indistinct conversation

MUSIC:
almost absent initially.

VOICEOVER:
“This is Dan. He’s a pretty normal kid.”

Dan laughs with his friends.

VOICEOVER:
“Except there’s a little more going on in his hair than he knows.”

The camera slowly begins moving closer to Dan.

Do not make the transition sudden.

Move gradually:
wide / medium →
closer →
hair beginning to dominate frame.

VOICEOVER:
“A lot more.”

This is the final narration in Part 1.

Narrator performance:
- warm
- dry
- understated
- natural
- conversational
- not a trailer voice
- not a theatrical storybook voice

==================================================
SCENE 2 — ENTERING THE WORLD
==================================================

The camera moves into Dan’s hair.

This is a major signature visual moment for the series.

IMPORTANT WORLD RULE:

Hair and scalp are the hidden biological origin of the world, not the visible aesthetic.

Do NOT depict:
- literal medical scalp
- pores
- grease
- inflamed skin
- disgusting flakes
- dermatological close-up imagery

Instead, transform scale into the established fantastical D&D world.

As the camera descends:
- hair-inspired fibres become enormous vertical structures
- particles move through atmosphere
- distant lights appear
- bridges and pathways become visible
- glimpses of neighbourhoods appear
- tiny citizens and transport may be seen
- Head Centre exists far below

Do not turn this into a long sightseeing montage.

The audience should get flashes of a much larger world.

CAMERA:
fluid descent
controlled
cinematic
motivated by the scale transition

Avoid generic flying AI camera movement.

==================================================
SOUND TRANSITION
==================================================

The sound should sell the scale transformation.

As the camera approaches Dan’s hair:

Human-world sounds become:
- distant
- muffled
- slower-feeling
- deeper in frequency

A normal football impact or footstep may become a distant enormous boom.

During descent, gradually introduce:
- fibre movement
- wind-like resonance
- tiny bells
- distant voices
- transport
- mechanical sounds
- subtle city ambience

The transition must feel seamless.

By the time the Scalpence Centre is reached, the hidden-world soundscape fully replaces the human world.

==================================================
MUSIC TRANSITION
==================================================

Introduce the recurring Dan & the Dandruffs musical identity during the descent.

Musical language:
- tactile
- curious
- slightly eccentric
- warm
- subtle plucked instruments
- small woodwinds
- unusual light percussion
- memorable but restrained motif

Avoid:
- generic Disney music
- generic whimsical pizzicato
- sentimental piano
- heroic orchestral scoring
- fantasy cliché

This motif should be reusable across future episodes.

As the Scalpence Centre approaches, add slightly more rhythmic energy.

==================================================
GENERAL ARROW ENTERS
==================================================

General Arrow enters with slightly excessive authority.

He takes himself seriously.

Optional subtle comedy:
he nearly bumps into a worker or object, corrects himself immediately, and continues as if nothing happened.

Do not make it slapstick.

GENERAL ARROW
“What’s the Boy’s status?”

Visible dialogue must be accurately lip-synced.

A Scalpetist looks up from the workstation.

SCALPETIST
“He’s finished school. He’s heading to the Green Field with his friends.”

General Arrow reacts immediately.

He turns toward the room.

GENERAL ARROW
“Alright, everyone. Listen up!”

The room gradually focuses on him.

==================================================
GENERAL ARROW’S ORDERS
==================================================

Do NOT keep the whole speech in one static shot.

Use professional dialogue coverage and visual storytelling.

GENERAL ARROW
“The Boy is heading to the Green Field.”

Cut to workers reacting.

GENERAL ARROW
“Get someone on every telescope.”

SHOW:
workers moving into position.

A large world-native telescope turns.

GENERAL ARROW
“Watch for contaminated Heads.”

SHOW:
an observer scanning the upper world.

Do not fully reveal everything yet.

GENERAL ARROW
“Issue a wet-soil warning.”

SHOW:
a worker activates a physical warning mechanism.

This may involve:
- lever
- bell
- mechanical indicator
- rotating signal
- analogue warning device

GENERAL ARROW
“And recommend all elders remain indoors tonight.”

Activity increases.

General Arrow becomes slightly more serious.

GENERAL ARROW
“The Head will be shaking, and I want every possible Long Foot threat contained.”

At “Long Foot”:
slightly reduce ambient noise.

Let the phrase carry weight.

GENERAL ARROW
“Understood?”

Brief beat.

SCALPETISTS
“Sir, yes, sir!”

They respond with energy, but not robotic perfect synchronisation.

Immediately:
the room returns to activity.

Instruments move.
Warnings begin.
Observers take position.

LIP SYNC
==================================================

All visible dialogue must be synced accurately to final audio.

Do NOT accept:
- generic mouth flapping
- mouths moving after dialogue ends
- bad phoneme matching
- disappearing teeth
- jaw deformation
- facial identity drift

Where reliable lip sync cannot be achieved:
edit around the problem professionally.

Use:
- over-the-shoulder shots
- listener reactions
- instrument inserts
- hands operating controls
- wider coverage
- profile angles

Do not leave obviously incorrect mouth movement in the final export.
