# 02 — CREATIVE NOTES

The script works. The central device (body-wipe to the same angle years later), the "Yeah, obviously" line, the not-sad smile and the sound-only ending are all kept as written. Notes below are only the ones that change something.

## DECISIONS NEEDING THE CREATOR

### 1. Runtime: the script plays at ~34 s, not 30 s
The script's own timecodes are optimistic. Eight spoken lines, a match cut that needs a hold, two memories and a final shot with a pause do not fit in 30.0 s without rushing the adult section, which is the part that needs room.

- **Planned (recommended): 0:34.0.** The childhood half stays fast; the adult half gets room.
- **0:30 trim, ready if you want it** (−3.9 s):
  - drop `S02_SH02` shoulder-barge (−1.0) — also removes the riskiest shot in the film
  - drop `S03_SH02` backpack-grab insert (−0.8) — loses the "one goalpost left" image
  - merge `S06_SH03` + `S06_SH04` into one 1.5 s shot (−1.0)
  - shorten `S04_SH02` to 1.7, `S05_SH01` to 2.2, `S07_SH01` to 3.8 (−1.1)

  Cost of the trim: the reveal hold and the final pause both get tighter.

### 2. Aspect ratio assumed 16:9
The fence-line wides and the locked ANGLE A are composed for 16:9. If this is a vertical Short (9:16), ANGLE A, the establishing shot and the final shot need recomposing before `/assets`.

## SMALL DECISIONS MADE (no approval needed, listed for transparency)

**a. "You're joking!" is cut.** Three back-and-forth lines inside a 1.2 s shot become noise. "That wasn't in!" / "Course it was!" plays off-screen over the ball rolling between the bags, then laughter. The argument is still there.

**b. Almost all dialogue is off-screen or back-to-camera.** Only one line is lip-synced: Sam's "You coming tomorrow?". It is the line the film turns on, and it is the one face the audience must remember. Every other line is shouted across a wide, over an insert, or from a turned head.

**c. The flash is the same clip, not a new one.** `S05_SH03` reuses `S03_SH03` exactly. A memory that replays identically is more convincing than a re-staged one, and it costs nothing.

**d. "The empty spot" is the same framing as Sam's shot.** `S05_SH04` is Sam's exact frame, present day, with no Sam and no ball. The viewer does the comparison themselves.

**e. One goalpost left.** When Leo grabs his backpack (`S03_SH02`), Sam's bag stays on the grass alone. The goal is already broken before anyone knows the game is over.

**f. How the audience knows it is the same person.** No text, so it is carried by three things: the tuft of hair at his crown and his ears; a colour echo (boy's mustard T-shirt, adult's ochre jumper under a dark coat); and a habit — he hitches a bag strap up his right shoulder with his thumb, as a boy in `S03_SH04` and as a man in `S05_SH01`.

**g. He exits the same way twice.** The boy runs home out of frame right. The man walks out of frame right in the last shot.

**h. The gateway is still open in the present.** No chain, no padlock. He could walk in. He does not. A locked gate would make it about loss; an open one keeps it about choice, which matches "not sad".

**i. The present is cool and flat, not bleak.** Overcast late afternoon. No rain, no dead trees, no litter. The trees are bigger and healthy. The place is simply no longer used.

**j. The shoulder-barge is restaged as a single.** Two bodies colliding is the likeliest shot to fail. `S02_SH02` frames Leo alone; a shoulder comes in from the frame edge and he stumbles. Same beat.

**k. Music is minimal and absent at both ends.** No score in the childhood half (voices, ball, breath). No score in the final shot (the script's faint memory sounds need silence around them). At most a sparse two-note motif under the two memories. Fully specified at `/edit`; you may prefer no music at all.
