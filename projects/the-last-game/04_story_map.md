# 04 — STORY MAP

Three-act structure does not fit a 34-second film. It has two halves and a hinge.

| Section | Time | Dur | Narrative purpose | Emotional state | Visual energy | Audience question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **THEN 1 — Arrival** (S1) | 0:00.0–0:04.8 | 4.8 | Establish the place, the friends, the backpack goal | Eager, at home | High. Running, low sun, quick cuts | What game is this, and who are these kids? |
| **THEN 2 — The game** (S2) | 0:04.8–0:09.6 | 4.8 | Show how ordinary and complete this is | Careless joy | Highest. Fragments, feet, handheld feeling | (none — the viewer is simply inside it) |
| **THEN 3 — Called home** (S3) | 0:09.6–0:15.1 | 5.5 | Plant the promise | Casual certainty | Slows. Longer shots, first held face | Why is the film stopping on this small exchange? |
| **THE CUT** (S4) | 0:15.1–0:18.5 | 3.4 | Deliver the premise in one cut | — (viewer's shock, not the character's) | Drops to zero. Locked frame, nothing moves | What happened? How long has it been? |
| **NOW 1 — Recognition** (S5) | 0:18.5–0:25.3 | 6.8 | He remembers the promise; viewer learns it was never kept | Preoccupied → recognition | Still. One slow walk, one face, one empty frame | Did he ever come back? |
| **NOW 2 — Second memory** (S6) | 0:25.3–0:29.7 | 4.4 | Turn the feeling from loss to fondness | Fondness | Brief burst of memory, then stillness | Is he going to go in? |
| **NOW 3 — Walking on** (S7) | 0:29.7–0:34.0 | 4.3 | He keeps it and leaves | Acceptance | One locked shot | (answered: no — and that is fine) |

The contrast is the storytelling. The first half must be loud and fast so the second half reads as quiet.
