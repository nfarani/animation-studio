# 17 — HIGH-RISK SHOT FALLBACKS

Four HIGH-RISK shots. Each fallback keeps the story beat. Attempt cap: 4, then move down the list.

---

## S02_SH02 — the shoulder-barge (1.0 s)
**Beat to preserve:** the game is rough and friendly; Leo nearly goes over and loves it.

**WHY HIGH RISK:** character-to-character contact at speed; a stumble (fast whole-body motion) with a readable face.

**PRIMARY:** medium on Leo; Danny's green shoulder enters from frame right and bumps him; Leo lurches, recovers, grins.

**FALLBACK A:** Leo alone in the same framing. He lurches left as if shoved; the contact is off-frame and carried by a thud and a grunt. No second body in the shot.

**FALLBACK B:** low angle on feet only: two pairs of legs converge, one pair staggers sideways, a hand touches the grass and pushes off.

**CHEAPER ALTERNATIVE:** drop the shot. Scene 2 runs feet → goal → chase and loses nothing essential. This is also the first cut in the 0:30 trim.

---

## S03_SH03 — "You coming tomorrow?" (1.8 s, used twice)
**Beat to preserve:** Sam, standing still beside the ball, asks. The audience must remember his face and where he stood.

**WHY HIGH RISK:** the only lip-synced line; precise, restrained performance; locked camera required; any flaw is seen twice.

**PRIMARY:** full shot, ANGLE B, static. Sam with his right foot on the ball, asks, waits.

**FALLBACK A — voice leads the picture:** start the line over the tail of S03_SH02 (the navy bag alone), then cut to Sam on the last word, mouth just closing, eyebrows up, waiting. Only the final syllable needs to match. The flash (S05_SH03) then uses the same picture with the full line laid over it; in a 1.2 s memory flash nobody checks sync.

**FALLBACK B — over the shoulder:** camera behind Sam's right shoulder, looking at Leo backing away. Sam's mouth is not visible. The flash becomes a new, silent, front-on shot of Sam standing with his foot on the ball (no speech needed), with the line as sound. Costs one extra standard shot and merges S03_SH03 with S03_SH04's geography.

**CHEAPER ALTERNATIVE:** drop the lip-sync pass. Generate Sam with a natural "speaking" mouth movement of about the right length and cut the audio to it by hand. At full-shot scale and 1.8 s this is frequently good enough. Try this **before** paying for a lip-sync pass.

---

## S04_SH01 — the body wipe (1.4 s)
**Beat to preserve:** Leo leaves toward us; his body covers the frame; when it clears, years have passed.

**WHY HIGH RISK:** the camera must be perfectly still; a running figure approaching to extreme close range distorts; the frame must be fully covered for a clean cut.

**PRIMARY:** ANGLE A, Leo runs from mid-pitch through the gateway and past the lens on its right; mustard shirt fills the frame.

**FALLBACK A — finish it in the edit:** generate only the approach, to the point where Leo is a medium shot. In the edit, scale and blur the last frames into a mustard wipe (or use a separate 6-frame element: a blurred mustard shape crossing right to left). The audience reads it as his body.

**FALLBACK B — backpack wipe:** Leo swings the red backpack onto his shoulder as he passes; the red bag crosses the lens. A separate economy element: a blurred red shape wiping the frame. The cut then goes red → grey, which is a stronger colour change than mustard → grey.

**CHEAPER ALTERNATIVE:** no wipe. Leo runs out of frame right at mid-distance; hold the THEN frame empty of him for 8 frames (Sam small in the background); **hard cut** to the NOW frame. Less elegant than the script's device but the match cut still lands because the frame is locked.

---

## S06_SH04 — hand on the fence (1.3 s)
**Beat to preserve:** he touches the fence for a second and takes his hand away.

**WHY HIGH RISK:** hand interaction with a fine repeating mesh in macro; fingers merge with wire; finger count errors are obvious at this scale.

**PRIMARY:** macro, hand already resting on the far side of the wire; fingers press; hand lifts away; wire springs back.

**FALLBACK A — wide from behind:** ANGLE A. Leo at the fence, back three-quarter to camera; his right arm comes up, the hand rests on the wire at shoulder height, comes down again. The hand is a few pixels tall. This can be the opening 1.3 s of S07_SH01, generated as one longer take.

**FALLBACK B — the wire without the hand:** macro of the chain-link only. It presses in a few millimetres as if leaned on, holds, and springs back. A soft charcoal shape out of focus behind it. The sound of the wire does the rest.

**CHEAPER ALTERNATIVE:** fold the gesture into S06_SH03: at the bottom of the close-up frame, the wire visibly flexes toward camera as he smiles. No hand in shot. This is the version used in the 0:30 trim.
